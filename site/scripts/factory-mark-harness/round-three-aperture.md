## What the rendered comparison revealed

- The square recursive construction is distinctive and reads at 48 px.
- It is too static, too much like a maze or square spiral, and slightly bottom-heavy.
- The terminal horizontal exit is useful, but the whole mark needs more directional energy.
- The teal seam currently feels applied after the geometry was finished. It must become a load-bearing aperture or transition.
- The separate filled core plus attached stroke creates an awkward lump at small size. Resolve it optically.

## Produce exactly three refinements

All three must remain recognizably descended from Recursive Aperture, not restart the concept exploration.

1. **09 Dynamic Aperture** — introduce a measured diagonal, clipped corner, or asymmetric step so the recursion has forward energy. Keep the silhouette compact and the exit unmistakable.
2. **10 Continuous Gate** — make the teal segment a literal part of the single path: the color transition should mark where bounded work crosses the outer boundary. Avoid an added-on perpendicular tick.
3. **11 Reduced Aperture** — remove one turn or one enclosure and rebalance the remaining geometry for the most logo-like, least diagram-like result. It must still communicate recursion/bounded execution and a verified exit.

## Hard constraints

- Return three complete standalone SVG strings plus a concise rationale for each.
- Exact `viewBox="0 0 640 420"`; no width or height attributes.
- Dark-ground palette only: warm white `#dde4e1`, teal `#4fdbc8`.
- Transparent background, no text, no wordmark, no gradients, no filters, no shadows, no animation.
- Use only SVG geometry; no embedded raster data or external resources.
- Maximum 5 visible geometry elements per candidate, excluding `title` and `desc`.
- Prefer one continuous primary path. Keep strokes visually consistent and use rounded joins/caps where they improve optical balance.
- Provide unique accessible `title` and `desc` IDs in each SVG.
- It must remain legible at 32–48 px and look deliberate at 640 × 420.
- Avoid literal arrows, check marks, gears, circuit-board decoration, padlocks, browser-window metaphors, letterforms, and a generic infinity silhouette.
- Do not create a maze puzzle. The path hierarchy and the crossing through verification must be understood immediately.

Judge the three as identity marks, not explanatory diagrams. The result should feel rigorous, calm, and slightly surprising.
